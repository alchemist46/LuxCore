Bugs and issues found during a task that were not fixed right away. See "Keep the bug list" in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) for the rules. Remove an entry when it is fixed.

The first entries were collected on 2026-10-20 from "Known open issues" in [MODERNIZATION_PLAN.md](docs/history/MODERNIZATION_PLAN.md) (the "Found" date is the date given there) and from [SPEEDUP_CHANGES.md](docs/history/SPEEDUP_CHANGES.md); the plan has the full measurements. Entries the plan already marks as fixed are not listed. The open work items are in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). Bugs in the BlendLuxCore addon are in the addon's own `BUGS.md`.

### PATHOCL: a render halted by spp is darker or brighter by the samples in flight
- Found: 2026-11-03
- Area: engine (PATHOCL)
- Problem: the image mean of a PATHOCL render halted by `batch.haltspp` depends on the number of GPU tasks, by a term that falls as 1/spp: the samples still in flight at the halt are left out, and they are more often the long paths. Plain cornell (perspective, field of view 70, 480x320, SOBOL, adaptive sampling off, 64 spp): 0.3058 and 0.3057 with the default task count against 0.3064 and 0.3063 with `opencl.task.count = 65536` (seeds 3, 4), 0.2%. A scene whose paths have very different lengths shows it most: a fisheye inside the cornell box (`scene.camera.type = realistic`, `lens.preset = fisheye`, half of the frame outside its image circle, whose rays end at their first vertex since 2026-11-03) is 1.5% darker at 64 spp, 0.35% at 256 and 0.08% at 1024 than the CPU (PATHCPU 1024 spp: 0.7343 on the pixels inside the circle), and the same as the CPU with 65536 tasks.
- Reproduce: the two renders above, PATHOCL on HIP (RX 6950 XT), `film.opencl.enable = 0`, compare the means; with adaptive sampling on the per-pixel sample counts spread too (211-435 at 256 spp).
- Notes: the CPU engines have one sample in flight per thread, too few to show it. To check: whether the halt drops the in-flight samples or stops the tasks mid-path; finishing the samples in flight (or not counting the ones started after the target) would remove it.

### Camera: the custom image bokeh is not centered on the lens axis
- Found: 2026-11-02
- Area: camera
- Problem: with `scene.camera.bokeh.distribution.type = CUSTOM`, `PerspectiveCamera::SampleAperture()` (and `PerspectiveCamera_SampleAperture()` on the GPU) take the lens point as the image sample `uv` times the bokeh scale, with `uv` in [0, 1]^2 (`Distribution2D::SampleContinuous()`): the aperture is the square from the lens center to (1, 1) lens radii, off the axis, and smaller than the other distributions' (radius 1). The out-of-focus parts of the image shift with their defocus, as through an off-center aperture.
- Reproduce: a small emitting dot out of focus (lens radius 0.1, focus 5, the dot at 2.5) with a custom bokeh image of a centered disk, against `UNIFORM` with 0 blades: the bokeh moves away from the dot's in-focus position. Read from the code (F11, 2026-11-02), not rendered yet.
- Notes: the expected mapping is `(2 uv - 1)` times the scale. Fixing it changes the images of the scenes that use a custom bokeh; the optical vignetting table (F11) samples the aperture the same way, so it follows.

### Camera: the stereo camera's bokeh is not compiled for the GPU
- Found: 2026-11-02

The CPU eyes are new `PerspectiveCamera`s with the default bokeh, so they ignore the scene's bokeh too.

### OpenPBR: coated glass reflects too much (the translucent Fresnel is relative to the exterior, not the coat)
- Found: 2026-11-01
- Area: material
- Problem: under a coat, the specification gives the base dielectric's Fresnel with `specular.ior` relative to the coat. The opaque base does that (`OpenPBRBSDF::eta`), but the translucent interface (`etaT`, `OpenPBR_TranslucentEta()` in `materialdefs_funcs_openpbr_bsdf.cl`, with dispersion too) uses the IOR relative to the exterior for both its Fresnel and its refraction. A glass of IOR 1.5 under a coat of IOR 1.6 (coat.weight 1, transmission.weight 1) gets a base F0 of 4.0% instead of about 0.1%: about 9% reflected head-on instead of about 5.4%. The code is not consistent with itself either: `OpenPBR_BaseAlbedo()` (its transmission part) and `OpenPBR_DeltaBelowCoat()` feed the coat darkening with `eta`, and the film over the translucent interface is relative to the coat while its substrate is relative to the exterior.
- Reproduce: bsdftest with `coat.weight 1`, `coat.ior 1.6`, `specular.ior 1.5`, `transmission.weight 1`, `specular.roughness 0`: the reflected albedo head-on.
- Notes: found by the OpenPBR review (2026-11-01). The fix separates the two: the coat-mixed relative IOR for the Fresnel and its energy tables (with the total internal reflection of the inside face), `etaT` only for the refracted direction (through parallel layers it depends only on the exterior and the base). The rough transmission's Jacobian and the albedo tables use one eta today, so it is not a one-line change.

### OpenPBR: the dispersion tints the whole BSDF, diffuse included
- Found: 2026-11-01
- Area: material
- Problem: with a path wavelength, `OpenPBR_Disperse()` tints the whole value of the vertex at any hit with `transmission.weight > 0` and dispersion on, the diffuse and the opaque reflection included. A mostly opaque material (transmission 0.05 with dispersion) gets per-sample colour noise on its diffuse. The mean is right (suspected, not measured): only the noise.
- Reproduce: an OpenPBR sphere with `transmission.weight 0.05`, `transmission.dispersionscale 1`, base colour white, under an area light, PATHOCL 64 spp: colour noise on the diffuse.
- Notes: found by the OpenPBR review (2026-11-01). The opaque part (`eta`) does not depend on the wavelength; tinting only the translucent part needs the path's `dispersionTinted` handling to know which lobe was sampled.

### OpenPBR: speed items left from the review
- Found: 2026-11-01
- Area: material
- Problem: work done more than once, measured by reading the code only:
  - `coat.bumptex` is bumped again on every `GetBSDF` (Sample, each light Evaluate, EvaluateDiffuse, Albedo): about 3 texture evaluations each time, with 6 stores and restores of the hit point on the GPU and a copy of the HitPoint on the CPU. Albedo does not need the coat normal.
  - In Evaluate (eye path), Sample and EvaluateDiffuse, `OpenPBR_LobeWeights()` and `OpenPBR_EvaluateFront()` do the same albedo-table and sheen LTC lookups for the same eye direction (about 7 lookups). Tried on 2026-11-02 (a struct of the eye direction's terms, computed once, bit-identical on the CPU): no gain on HIP (plastic 6.01 -> 6.00 ms per pass) and 96 bytes more scratch (Matte +1%), so not kept; the CPU was not measured.
  - The thin film over a metal (`CalcFilmColorConductor()`) runs 3 full 34-wavelength loops and keeps one channel of each; one loop with the 3 substrates sharing the phase would be about 3x cheaper.
  - `OpenPBR_EvaluateDiffuse()` (PhotonGI's diffuse split) still takes the OpenPBRBSDF struct by pointer in a call (GetBSDF and EvaluateFront are inlined since 2026-11-02, see below); about 24 dwords of the struct are read only by `OpenPBR_Init()` (an input/derived split).
  - With a coat normal, `OpenPBR_CoatBasis()` is rebuilt in every `ToCoatFrame`/`FromCoatFrame` (5-7 times per call).
  - The CPU `Pdf()` runs the whole Evaluate (film loops included) for the pdfs.
- Measured after the fixes (2026-11-02): see the end of the notes. Before them (2026-11-02, 82d17bcfb): OpenPBR against the legacy material that looks most like it, on the cube and the sphere of the simple test scene (walls matte), time per pass ratio legacy/OpenPBR on HIP (PATHOCL) / CPU (PATHCPU): matte 0.85 / 0.89, plastic (Glossy2) 0.89 / 1.03, metal (Metal2 gold) 0.83 / 0.96, smooth glass 0.91 / 0.92, rough glass 0.74 / 0.74 (OpenPBR's paths are longer: Fresnel choice instead of 50/50), coated (GlossyCoating over Matte) 1.11 / 0.93. With the noise at equal spp (efficiency = 1 / (variance x time) on the objects): matte 0.85 / 0.89, plastic 1.07 / 1.30, metal 0.95 / 1.08, glass 1.99 / 2.04, rough glass 3.87 / 4.30, coated 1.38 / 1.32. So the plain cost shows where OpenPBR has no noise advantage: as a Lambert diffuse (same image and noise as Matte) it costs 18% more time per pass on HIP, 12% on the CPU.
- Reproduce: n/a for the code-reading items; the measurement: `C:\Users\Ricardo\lc-work\opbr\matspeed.py PATHOCL 256 2` (and `PATHCPU 128 2`), with the pyluxcore venv's python.
- Notes: found by the OpenPBR review (2026-11-01). Each one needs its variance x time (or samples per second) measured, and the kernel's `[AdvancePaths_MK_RT] ... bytes of scratch` log line checked. Not measured yet: which part of a call costs the diffuse case (GetBSDF's input reads, OpenPBR_Init(), the lobe weights of the lobes that are off, the struct in scratch on HIP, two GetBSDF + Init per vertex for Evaluate and Sample).
  Measured and fixed on 2026-11-02 (kernel variants of the plastic set-up, each running one part twice): the BSDF arithmetic is cheap (Evaluate twice +0.05 ms per pass, Sample twice +0.14, OpenPBR_Init() twice +0.22), the cost was OpenPBRBSDF going through scratch memory into the not inlined GetBSDF and EvaluateFront (both inlined: -11%). With that, the lobe mask (the inputs and the Init() terms of the parts that are off skipped) and the diffuse-only path, OpenPBR's time per pass on HIP against the legacy material: matte 0.97 (was 1.18), plastic 0.97-0.98 vs Glossy2 (was 1.11), metal 1.05 vs Metal2 (was 1.18), coated -11% and rough glass -9% against the previous OpenPBR, CPU renders bit-identical (the diffuse-only path within 3e-7). Left: metal about 5% behind Metal2 (its F82 Fresnel and energy compensation per channel).

### BiDir blends the toon material's steps with its Lambert look
- Found: 2026-11-01
- Area: material (toon), engine (BIDIRCPU)
- Problem: the toon material's direct light is stepped in `Evaluate()` and its BSDF sampling is the Lambert lobe of kd; the path tracers take the whole direct light of a toon vertex from the light sampling (`BSDF::IsDirectLightSamplingOnly()`), BiDir does not, so its connections and its MIS mix the two looks. Furnace `toon-ramp` (expected 1.4): PATHCPU 1.4002, PATHOCL 1.4008, BIDIRCPU 1.114. Light tracing (LIGHTCPU, the hybrid back/forward caustics) sees `Evaluate()` at the camera connections only.
- Reproduce: `tests/furnace/furnace.py BIDIRCPU toon spp=64 env=inf`.
- Notes: by design the toon material is not a BSDF, so BiDir has no consistent estimator for it; the same rule (a connection at a toon eye vertex counted with weight 1 only for the light sampling strategy) would make BiDir agree with the path tracers. A toon inside a Mix, a glossy coating or a two-sided material gets the same blend on the path tracers too: only a top-level toon gets the rule (the Mix's pdf is the sum of its sub-materials', so the rule would need the toon's share of it). With point, spot and distant lights there is no MIS, so the steps stay sharp in every case. BiDir and a nested toon also ignore `lightcenter` (they evaluate the sampled direction; the light-centre direction is only in the path tracers' light sampling, `BSDF::EvaluateToon()`), so area lights blur their steps. A consistent BSDF (MoonRay's toon diffuse: the stepped lobe sampled and weighted as a BSDF) would fix both, but can't have `lightcenter`: a BSDF hit on a light would need the same value as the light sample of its centre.

### A glossycoating over a material without glossy lobes reports glossiness 0 (its glossy bounces count as nearly specular)
- Found: 2026-10-31
- Area: material
- Problem: `GlossyCoatingMaterial`'s constructor sets `glossiness = Min(ComputeGlossiness(nu, nv), matBase->GetGlossiness())` whatever the base, and a base without glossy lobes (matte, roughmatte) has glossiness 0, so the coating reports 0 however rough it is; `UpdateTextureReferences()` takes the base into account only when it has glossy lobes (as `MixMaterial::Preprocess()` does), so the value depends on whether a texture edit happened. Glossiness 0 makes every glossy bounce on the coating "nearly specular" (`PathInfo::IsNearlySpecular()`): the PhotonGI caches (no indirect cache at the next vertex, the caustic chain), hybrid back/forward and the shadow transparency rule (`IsSpecularPathAfter()`) treat a 0.3-rough coating as a mirror.
- Reproduce: a `glossycoating` with `uroughness = 0.3` over a `matte`: `GetGlossiness()` returns 0 after `Scene.Parse()`, 0.3 after any texture edit of the material.
- Notes: the fix is the constructor doing what `UpdateTextureReferences()` does; it moves the images of every scene with a coated material on these paths, so it needs its own parity and bias check. The PhotonGI glossy cutoff (CA4 follow-up) reads `GetGlossyLobesGlossiness()`, which leaves the base out already.

### WaitNewFrame(generation, timeoutMs) sees the CPU engines' samples one Windows timer tick late
- Found: 2026-10-30
- Area: engine (film generation, ROADMAP E6 (c))
- Problem: for the engines that don't notify their film updates (every engine but RTPATHOCL), a wait polls the film sample count every 5 ms, and on Windows a 5 ms `condition_variable::wait_until()` sleeps a whole 15.6 ms timer tick. ### GPU Metropolis: short chains still read dark on some scenes
- Found: 2026-10-17 (as "GPU Metropolis sampler bias with short chains"), narrowed on 2026-10-26 by ROADMAP GM; left as a known limitation (the user, 2026-10-26: Metropolis is not used on the GPU, GM dropped)
- Area: engine (PATHOCL, Metropolis sampler)
- Problem: since GM the chains start from the bootstrap, share one luminance estimate and splat their last sample at the halt, which removed most of the bias, but short chains still read dark on some scenes: PATHOCL against the Sobol mean, 4 seeds, at 16/64/256 spp: the `simple` scene (150x100) -1.0/-0.5/-0.2% (with 65536 tasks -1.3/-1.4/-0.6%), LynxSpider (300x200) -2.4/-1.2/-0.05%; the SDL `cornell` (+0.4/+0.1/0.0%) and `luxball-hdr` (-0.1/0.0/0.0%) are within the noise from 64 spp. A chain gets about spp x pixels / tasks samples (one task per pixel on a small film).
