## 1. Where the engine stands

Against Cycles on the same RX 6950 XT: **3.4x** at equal error on the new hand-made Cornell pair of `scenes/cornell` (one scene per engine, 2026-10-24, 540x540, 12/4/4/12 bounces as the files have them; time per sample 2.8x, variance per sample 1.19x; `g0_prepare.py --native`, then `g0_run.sh` with `<cycles.blend>,<luxcore.blend>`; the two files match in brightness within 1%), the former G0 scene being **2.6x** on the Blender Cornell box (D3 re-measure of 2026-10-23 on Windows, 540x540, depth 8; 3.3x before D3, 4.5x in the G0 re-measure of 2026-10-17), **8.6x** on the Blender classroom (2026-10-17). The two factors:

| | Cornell box | classroom |
|---|---|---|
| time per sample, PATHOCL / Cycles | 3.4x | ~3x |
| variance per sample, PATHOCL / Cycles | 0.76x | ~0.85x |

The trace is not the limit (203M rays/s on the 32-triangle Cornell box, 187M on the 247k-triangle classroom). Textured scenes lose on the shading of a vertex; the Cornell box at full depth loses on the time per sample (1.4x with direct light only, 3.9x at depth 8 without its glass and metal), and its direct light alone, 4-10x Cycles' noise on the walls with the area light sampled as two triangles, is at 0.9x/2x at 64/256 spp since D3 (the rest is the dimension pairs; padding them as Cycles does, D3b, loses at full depth and was dropped). The fixed cost of a render is 0.85-4.3 s against 0-0.3 s for Cycles. The cold kernel compile is solved on Windows (G9).

Done, for orientation: CI with tests and the benchmark set; S0 (the kernel timing and the textures' share); A3; B1 (the hit point diet; the lazy `dndu/dndv` measured and dropped); B3 (the learning stop); B4; E3; the texture half of G11b; Owen-scrambled Sobol with blue-noise pixel decorrelation; the per-pixel adaptive halt (as an option); GGX/VNDF and energy compensation always on; the material standards pass; random-walk subsurface; OpenPBR; the Arnold/Cycles path depths; the working colour space; the light tree as the default strategy; G1-G5, G7, G10, G11; G9 on Windows; C1-C5; F1-F3; P1-P3; RANDOM sampler removed; CA2 (the DLS cache removed, the tree in its place).

## 2. Conventions

Entry: **ID. Title** `[tag]` — what, why, where in the code, how it is checked, dependencies, size. Tags: none = not started, `[PARTIAL]`, `[BLOCKED]`, `[MEASURE]` (measurement only), `[OPTION]` (implemented as an opt-in; the default is the open decision).

Measurement rules (from [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) and the speedup plan):
- GPU (PATHOCL on HIP) by default; CPU engines only when a test needs them. Short runs first.
- Noise without a reference: two seeds, `mean((a-b)^2)/2`, at 64 and 256 spp. A reference (~1024 spp) only for bias.
- **Bit-identical items:** TILEPATHOCL, fixed seed, `tilepath.sampling.aa.size` 8, no multipass; compare to 1e-7 against the previous build on cornell, luxball-hdr, classroom, kitchen, luxball-hdr-vol, cornell-vol, transparencyball, cornell-invis, the shadow-catcher cornell and luxball-shadowcatcher, plus the OpenCL device.
- **Statistical items:** seed pairs and equal means on the GPU.
- **Speed:** `tests/benchmark/renderbench.py`, old and new builds alternating, rays/s on Windows (±1%); the `[AdvancePaths_MK_RT] VGPRs / scratch` log line before and after. Per kernel: `tests/benchmark/kerneltiming.py` (`LUXRAYS_HIP_KERNEL_TIMING=1`); the G0 Blender classroom as a pyluxcore scene: `tests/benchmark/g0_export.py` (`--flat-textures` for the textures' share).
- **Tests:** `pyunittests/unittests.py --filter OCL` on the HIP device (known failure: `EnvironmentCamera` TILEPATHOCL at the threshold). Materials: `tests/furnace/`, `tests/bsdf/bsdftest`, CPU/GPU parity in each working space.
- After each commit: the BlendLuxCore check and the addon rebuild/install of `CLAUDE.md`; unresolved findings go to `BUGS.md`.

## 3. Tracks

### T1. GPU time per sample (PATHOCL)

Tiers A-C of the speedup plan plus the structural leftovers of the plan. Today: one `AdvancePaths_MK_RT` kernel per iteration, 256 VGPRs, ~2.5 KB scratch, 4 waves per SIMD; it is 86-99% of the GPU time, the HIPRT trace the rest (S0, 2026-10-21). On the G0 Blender classroom the image maps are 20% of the time per sample (31% of an iteration), and even without them a sample costs 5x the repo classroom's. Where a vertex spends its time: three interpreter walks per hit (volumes and pass-through), each texture evaluated 2-3 times per vertex, software bilinear image lookups, a 128-byte `Transform` copy per hit, N+1 wavefront iterations for a path of N vertices, repeated triangle-light work. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES.md "Key facts".

- **A1. Evaluate each texture once per vertex** `[DROPPED]` for the image maps. Done: the bilinear footprint wrapped once per axis (-4.1% on the G0 classroom). With A2 every image map lookup done twice costs the G0 classroom +1.8% (415.3 -> 422.8 ns/sample, two rounds; +14% before A2), so a per-vertex cache of their values cannot pay for its private array. The material parameter record (Cycles' closure model, filled once per hit and shared by Evaluate, Sample and Albedo; the CPU twin is C6) is a different gain, the interpreter walks rather than the lookups: weigh it with Tier C. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "A1".
- **A2. Hardware texture objects on HIP and CUDA** `[PARTIAL]`. Done on HIP: two texture objects per image map (filtered, and point sampling for the single-texel readers: bombing and the image map bump) on a HIP array, their handles in the device's copy of the `ImageMap` descriptions; the raw pages are not uploaded when every image map has its textures (density grids and white wrap keep them: AMD reads a black border whatever its colour); `LUXRAYS_HIP_TEXTURES=0` turns it off. G0 Blender classroom -12% time per sample. Left: CUDA (`cuTexObjectCreate`, the same structure; no NVIDIA GPU here to test it); the white wrap through the hardware (a black border plus the weight of the outside texels). Prerequisite of F1. `src/luxrays/devices/hipdevice.cpp`, `deps/hipew`, `pathoclbaseoclthreadinit.cpp`, `imagemap_funcs.cl`, `imagemap_types.cl`; CUDA: `cudadevice.cpp`, `deps/cuew`.
- **A4. No-stack fast path for one-op materials** `[DROPPED]`. The private leaf stack on the global-stack kernel cost kitchen 27%; the register-argument form rewrites the stack interface of every material for the leaf materials of the Mix scenes only (the others already have G11's private stack). Revisit with Tier C. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "A4".
- **A5. Common subexpression sharing in the texture op compiler** `[PARTIAL]`. *Done for the bump ops on the GPU 2026-10-23:* inside one bump evaluation, an evaluation repeated at the same hit point (a Scale or Mix of bumps evaluates each child's bump, whose generic bump starts with the child's value, and then that value again) copies the result of the first one from a stack slot (`EVAL_SLOT_STORE_*` / `EVAL_SLOT_LOAD_*`, run by `Texture_Bump()`; `CompileTextureOps()` in `compiletextures.cpp`). G0 classroom 7403 -> 6803 texture ops, stack 21 -> 25 floats, 367.2 / 367.2 -> 291.1 / 291.3 ns/sample (-20.7%, PATHOCL 640x360, 64 spp, two rounds); the other scenes, VGPRs and scratch unchanged; bit-identical on the parity set and the G0 classroom. Left: the float and spectrum evaluations (7-8% of their ops repeated on the G0 classroom, so a few percent at most); the CPU textures (`ScaleTexture::Bump()`, `MixTexture::Bump()` repeat the same evaluations). *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "A5".
- **A6. Each sub-material's bump once per vertex** `[PARTIAL]`. *Done on the GPU 2026-10-23:* a per-vertex bump cache. `CompileMaterialOps()` gives each bumped sub-material of a Mix or GlossyCoating tree a slot (at most 32 per material), the setup ops carry the slots of the children they bump, and `Material_BumpSubMaterial()` keeps their shading normals (3 floats per slot) at the end of the task's material eval stack block, with the valid mask in `HitPoint` (cleared by `BSDF_Init()`). Bit-identical on the parity set, the G0 classroom and a new nested-Mix case (`parity.py` `mixbump`). G0 classroom 405.3 / 405.2 -> 366.1 / 367.3 ns/sample (-9.6%, PATHOCL 640x360, 64 spp, two rounds); the other scenes, VGPRs and scratch unchanged. Left: the CPU (`mixmat.cpp` `Evaluate`/`Sample`/`Pdf` and `glossycoating.cpp` bump the sub-materials per call; its `HitPoint` copies per sub-material would need a per-vertex cache keyed the same way). *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "A6".
- **B2. No extra iteration at the end of a path** `[DROPPED]`. Measured 2026-10-22 with an upper-bound probe (the last shadow ray of a path dropped, so the sample is splat and the next camera ray made in the same iteration; `LUXRAYS_KERNEL_LOAD_DIR`, PATHOCL 64 spp, two rounds): cornell 29.1 -> 29.0 ns/sample (-0.5%), classroom 64.4 -> 61.9 (-3.9%), kitchen 69.7 -> 67.1 (-3.6%). The real item keeps that shadow ray and splats its contribution from a pending record a separate film add, which has to agree with variance clamping, the adaptive half buffers, the AOV channels and Metropolis (whole-sample splats), so it would recover less than the bound: not worth it. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "B2".
- **B5. Triangle light sampling without repeats** `[DROPPED]`. Measured 2026-10-22 with an upper-bound probe: the light pick pdf of `DirectHitFiniteLight()` (the MIS weight of an emitter hit, which recomputes the learned slot probabilities and walks the tree) replaced by a constant. PATHOCL 64 spp, two rounds: cornell 29.1, classroom 64.5, manylights 7.6 ns/sample unchanged, kitchen 69.6 -> 67.0 (-3.7%). Recovering kitchen's share means storing the pick's slot probabilities (up to 16 floats) in the task state at every vertex of every learning scene, which the classroom would pay for nothing; removing the private `learned[]` array of `LightTree_SlotProbabilities()` changed nothing (the compiler had it). The solid-angle repeats are only in the opt-in `trianglelight.solidangle` mode (MC). *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "B5".
- **C. Occupancy re-assessment** (after A and B). *Re-run 2026-10-22 after B1 and A2 (PATHOCL 64 spp, two rounds): the kernel still sits at 256 VGPRs; `LUXRAYS_HIP_WAVES_PER_EU` 5 / 6 gives 192 / 160 VGPRs with more scratch and cornell 29.1 -> 34.4 / 31.7 ns/sample, classroom 64.5 -> 72.2 / 70-72, kitchen 69.7 -> 98.0 / 92.8: no free occupancy, what B1 removed was scratch, not live registers.* Re-run the `LUXRAYS_HIP_WAVES_PER_EU` sweep and the `-Rpass-analysis=kernel-resource-usage` dump (`hiprtc_remarks.py`). If the kernel then fits 5-6 waves without spilling, the gain is free. If not, the route left is the per-member SoA accessor refactor plus per-state kernels with queues: the old G6 (compaction), the G7 leftover and "material sorting" are this one item (Cycles' own tracker: compaction "not clearly a win" with few kernels per bounce). Multi-week; weigh it then, not now. *History:* PLAN G6, G7 ("rejected" paragraphs), G8 table.
- **G11b. Peak-depth bound of the material evaluation stack** `[PARTIAL]`. Done: the textures (peak depth of the op tree; the G0 Blender classroom 212 -> 21 floats, under the private 32). *The private variant for Mix scenes measured and dropped 2026-10-22:* kitchen (one Mix level, 86 floats by the sum) on private material stacks of 40 / 48 / 96 / 128 floats (`LUXRAYS_HIP_COMPILER_OPTIONS` defines, PATHOCL 64 spp, two rounds) 69.8 -> 80.0 / 85.3 / 90.4 / 90.3 ns/sample: a private stack that holds a Mix costs more scratch traffic than the global one saves (G11 measured +9.6% before G7 moved the BSDF and the temporaries to scratch). The existing 16/32-float variant still pays (forced global: cornell 29.1 -> 31.8, classroom 67.1 -> 69.7, luxball-hdr 15.5 -> 18.1). Left, for memory only: size the shared global material stacks by the peak instead of the sum (348 floats on the G0 classroom, 713 MB at 960x540; the ops pop their parameters, so it needs the pops and pushes of every op type); and the addon calling `RemoveUnusedMaterials()`. *History:* PLAN G11 "Left open" and G11b, G9 step 7.
- *Not pursued:* fused trace and shader execution reordering (OptiX-only, after the state shrink), per-scene kernel specialization (G4, measured and rejected), a private copy of the whole task state (G7, rejected).

The legacy factor ramp (`ramp.thresholds`, `ramp.factors`, the default 0 0.3 / 0.3 1) is grey keys, 0 below the first, with `shadowcolor` filling what it leaves; the colour ramp has no shadow fill. `lightcenter` (default on): the ramp and the toon highlight take the direction of the centre of the light (`LightSource::GetCenterDir()`, `Light_GetCenterDir()`: the position of point, spot, projection and sphere lights, the direction of sun and distant lights, the area-weighted centroid of the lamp of a triangle light, the connected part of the light triangles of its mesh through the vertices at the same position, `TriangleLight::emitterCenter`), so area lights give hard bands with soft shadows (Blender test: band edge strength 0.34 with it, 0.06 without, under a 4 m area light); environment lights keep the sampled direction. The toon highlight: the step of Cycles' Toon BSDF on the angle between the normal and the half vector, full within the angle where the GGX distribution of alpha = `specular.roughness`^2 (0.5, a texture) falls to half its peak, tan^2 = alpha^2 (sqrt 2 - 1) / (1 - sqrt 2 alpha^2) (`ToonMaterial_HighlightAngle()`: roughness 0.2 1.5 degrees, 0.5 9.6, 0.7 21, the whole hemisphere from 0.917), so the highlight has the size of a glossy one of the same roughness; `specular.smooth` 0.05 fades its edge; stretched by 1 + `specular.stretch.u`/`.v` along the U and V directions (anisotropic, so a stretched highlight bends into an arc away from the equator of a sphere, as anisotropic GGX does). A first rework had a `specular.model` switch between this step and a GGX reflection with an IOR; the user found the two modes confusing and chose the one highlight with a roughness, as MoonRay's toon specular. The rim step on the angle between the eye direction and the surface (`rim.size` 0.2, `rim.smooth` 0.05). `Sample()` is the Lambert lobe of kd. The path tracers (PATHCPU, TILEPATHCPU, PATHOCL, TILEPATHOCL, RTPATH*) take the whole direct light of the toon part from the light sampling (`BSDF::EvaluateToon()`, `BSDF_EvaluateToon()`, MIS weight 1, the `diffuseEval` term of the PhotonGI split), and a BSDF hit on a light after a toon vertex adds nothing (`lastBSDFPdfW` 0). - **F10. Physically based lens post-effects: spectral chromatic aberration and GLARE** (done 2026-11-02 on the CPU, both opt-in image pipeline plugins; left: GLARE on the GPU). Before it, `COLOR_ABERRATION` blended a shifted copy into each pixel, elliptical on non-square images and sampled at pixel corners, and `BLOOM` (kept as is) is a Gaussian with no tail, spikes, wavelength or f-stop.
  - *Spectral CA:* `film.imagepipelines.N.mode` = `SPECTRAL` (the default) or `LEGACY` (the old code, bit-identical to the previous build on the test scene). SPECTRAL magnifies each of `.samples` (16) wavelengths, at the centers of strata of 380-780 nm, about `.center` (0.5 0.5, isotropic in pixels, from the pixel centers) by `1 + sqrt(2) * amount * (n(l) - n_d) / (n_F - n_C)` with Cauchy's dispersion; each channel sums its own channel at the scaled positions, weighted by `GlassMaterial::WaveLengthColor()` normalized per channel (a flat image stays unchanged). The planned `.abbe` was dropped: with a two-term Cauchy the ratio depends on neither the IOR nor the Abbe number, so `.amount` alone sets the strength. A negative amount moves the blue inward. Only the first value of `.amount` is used. The sqrt(2) makes the F-C spread at the corner of a square image equal to the legacy copies' distance; the B and R centroids end up 1.22 times that apart (the legacy channel centroids 0.5). The table (magnification, 3 weights) is built at parse time in the colour space of the image at the plugin's place (Rec.709 after a display curve) and serialized (class version 3; older films load as LEGACY); the CPU (8 lanes, `GetBilinearSamples()`) and the HW kernel `ColorAberrationPlugin_ApplySpectral` read the same table. Veiling glare: `(1 + (r/r0)^2)^(-p/2)` with `.scatter.radius` r0 (0.001 of the diagonal) and `.scatter.falloff` p (3). Negative kernel values (the cut of the spectrum) are removed and the output clamped at 0. Cost (12 threads): 1600x1000 60 ms per refresh, 3840x2160 380 ms, plus 0.1-0.5 s to build the transfer functions at the first run or a size change.
