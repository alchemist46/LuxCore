## 1. Where the engine stands

Against Cycles on the same RX 6950 XT: **3.4x** at equal error on the new hand-made Cornell pair of `scenes/cornell` (one scene per engine, 2026-10-24, 540x540, 12/4/4/12 bounces as the files have them; time per sample 2.8x, variance per sample 1.19x; `g0_prepare.py --native`, then `g0_run.sh` with `<cycles.blend>,<luxcore.blend>`; the two files match in brightness within 1%), the former G0 scene being **2.6x** on the Blender Cornell box (D3 re-measure of 2026-10-23 on Windows, 540x540, depth 8; 3.3x before D3, 4.5x in the G0 re-measure of 2026-10-17), **8.6x** on the Blender classroom (2026-10-17). The two factors:

| | Cornell box | classroom |
|---|---|---|
| time per sample, PATHOCL / Cycles | 3.4x | ~3x |
| variance per sample, PATHOCL / Cycles | 0.76x | ~0.85x |

The trace is not the limit (203M rays/s on the 32-triangle Cornell box, 187M on the 247k-triangle classroom). Textured scenes lose on the shading of a vertex; the Cornell box at full depth loses on the time per sample (1.4x with direct light only, 3.9x at depth 8 without its glass and metal), and its direct light alone, 4-10x Cycles' noise on the walls with the area light sampled as two triangles, is at 0.9x/2x at 64/256 spp since D3 (the rest is the dimension pairs, D3b). The fixed cost of a render is 0.85-4.3 s against 0-0.3 s for Cycles. The cold kernel compile is solved on Windows (G9).

Done, for orientation: CI with tests and the benchmark set; S0 (the kernel timing and the textures' share); A3; B1 (the hit point diet; the lazy `dndu/dndv` measured and dropped); B3 (the learning stop); B4; E3; the texture half of G11b; Owen-scrambled Sobol with blue-noise pixel decorrelation; the per-pixel adaptive halt (as an option); GGX/VNDF and energy compensation always on; the material standards pass; random-walk subsurface; OpenPBR; the Arnold/Cycles path depths; the working colour space; the light tree as the default strategy; G1-G5, G7, G10, G11; G9 on Windows; C1-C5; F1-F3; P1-P3; RANDOM sampler removed; CA2 (the DLS cache removed, the tree in its place).

## 2. Conventions

Entry: **ID. Title** `[tag]` — what, why, where in the code, how it is checked, dependencies, size. Tags: none = not started, `[PARTIAL]`, `[BLOCKED]`, `[MEASURE]` (measurement only), `[OPTION]` (implemented as an opt-in; the default is the open decision).

Measurement rules (from [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) and the speedup plan):
- GPU (PATHOCL on HIP) by default; CPU engines only when a test needs them. Short runs first.
- Noise without a reference: two seeds, `mean((a-b)^2)/2`, at 64 and 256 spp. A reference (~1024 spp) only for bias.
- **Bit-identical items:** TILEPATHOCL, fixed seed, `tilepath.sampling.aa.size` 8, no multipass; compare to 1e-7 against the previous build on cornell, luxball-hdr, classroom, kitchen, luxball-hdr-vol, cornell-vol, transparencyball, cornell-invis, the shadow-catcher cornell and luxball-shadowcatcher, plus the OpenCL device.
- **Statistical items:** seed pairs and equal means on the GPU.
- **Speed:** `tests/benchmark/renderbench.py`, old and new builds alternating, rays/s on Windows (±1%); the `[AdvancePaths_MK_RT] VGPRs / scratch` log line before and after. Per kernel: `tests/benchmark/kerneltiming.py` (`LUXRAYS_HIP_KERNEL_TIMING=1`); the G0 Blender classroom as a pyluxcore scene: `tests/benchmark/g0_export.py` (`--flat-textures` for the textures' share).
- **Tests:** `pyunittests/unittests.py --filter OCL` on the HIP device (known failure: `EnvironmentCamera` TILEPATHOCL at the threshold). Materials: `tests/furnace/`, `tests/bsdf/bsdftest`, CPU/GPU parity in each working space.
- After each commit: the BlendLuxCore check and the addon rebuild/install of `CLAUDE.md`; unresolved findings go to `BUGS.md`.

## 3. Tracks

### T1. GPU time per sample (PATHOCL)

Tiers A-C of the speedup plan plus the structural leftovers of the plan. Today: one `AdvancePaths_MK_RT` kernel per iteration, 256 VGPRs, ~2.5 KB scratch, 4 waves per SIMD; it is 86-99% of the GPU time, the HIPRT trace the rest (S0, 2026-10-21). On the G0 Blender classroom the image maps are 20% of the time per sample (31% of an iteration), and even without them a sample costs 5x the repo classroom's. Where a vertex spends its time: three interpreter walks per hit (volumes and pass-through), each texture evaluated 2-3 times per vertex, software bilinear image lookups, a 128-byte `Transform` copy per hit, N+1 wavefront iterations for a path of N vertices, repeated triangle-light work. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES.md "Key facts".

- **A1. Evaluate each texture once per vertex** `[DROPPED]` for the image maps. Done: the bilinear footprint wrapped once per axis (-4.1% on the G0 classroom). With A2 every image map lookup done twice costs the G0 classroom +1.8% (415.3 -> 422.8 ns/sample, two rounds; +14% before A2), so a per-vertex cache of their values cannot pay for its private array. The material parameter record (Cycles' closure model, filled once per hit and shared by Evaluate, Sample and Albedo; the CPU twin is C6) is a different gain, the interpreter walks rather than the lookups: weigh it with Tier C. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "A1".
- **A2. Hardware texture objects on HIP and CUDA** `[PARTIAL]`. Done on HIP: two texture objects per image map (filtered, and point sampling for the single-texel readers: bombing and the image map bump) on a HIP array, their handles in the device's copy of the `ImageMap` descriptions; the raw pages are not uploaded when every image map has its textures (density grids and white wrap keep them: AMD reads a black border whatever its colour); `LUXRAYS_HIP_TEXTURES=0` turns it off. G0 Blender classroom -12% time per sample. Left: CUDA (`cuTexObjectCreate`, the same structure; no NVIDIA GPU here to test it); the white wrap through the hardware (a black border plus the weight of the outside texels). Prerequisite of F1. `src/luxrays/devices/hipdevice.cpp`, `deps/hipew`, `pathoclbaseoclthreadinit.cpp`, `imagemap_funcs.cl`, `imagemap_types.cl`; CUDA: `cudadevice.cpp`, `deps/cuew`.
- **A4. No-stack fast path for one-op materials** `[DROPPED]`. The private leaf stack on the global-stack kernel cost kitchen 27%; the register-argument form rewrites the stack interface of every material for the leaf materials of the Mix scenes only (the others already have G11's private stack). Revisit with Tier C. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "A4".
- **A5. Common subexpression sharing in the texture op compiler** `[PARTIAL]`. *Done for the bump ops on the GPU 2026-10-23:* inside one bump evaluation, an evaluation repeated at the same hit point (a Scale or Mix of bumps evaluates each child's bump, whose generic bump starts with the child's value, and then that value again) copies the result of the first one from a stack slot (`EVAL_SLOT_STORE_*` / `EVAL_SLOT_LOAD_*`, run by `Texture_Bump()`; `CompileTextureOps()` in `compiletextures.cpp`). G0 classroom 7403 -> 6803 texture ops, stack 21 -> 25 floats, 367.2 / 367.2 -> 291.1 / 291.3 ns/sample (-20.7%, PATHOCL 640x360, 64 spp, two rounds); the other scenes, VGPRs and scratch unchanged; bit-identical on the parity set and the G0 classroom. Left: the float and spectrum evaluations (7-8% of their ops repeated on the G0 classroom, so a few percent at most); the CPU textures (`ScaleTexture::Bump()`, `MixTexture::Bump()` repeat the same evaluations). *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "A5".
- **A6. Each sub-material's bump once per vertex** `[PARTIAL]`. *Done on the GPU 2026-10-23:* a per-vertex bump cache. `CompileMaterialOps()` gives each bumped sub-material of a Mix or GlossyCoating tree a slot (at most 32 per material), the setup ops carry the slots of the children they bump, and `Material_BumpSubMaterial()` keeps their shading normals (3 floats per slot) at the end of the task's material eval stack block, with the valid mask in `HitPoint` (cleared by `BSDF_Init()`). Bit-identical on the parity set, the G0 classroom and a new nested-Mix case (`parity.py` `mixbump`). G0 classroom 405.3 / 405.2 -> 366.1 / 367.3 ns/sample (-9.6%, PATHOCL 640x360, 64 spp, two rounds); the other scenes, VGPRs and scratch unchanged. Left: the CPU (`mixmat.cpp` `Evaluate`/`Sample`/`Pdf` and `glossycoating.cpp` bump the sub-materials per call; its `HitPoint` copies per sub-material would need a per-vertex cache keyed the same way). *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "A6".
- **B2. No extra iteration at the end of a path** `[DROPPED]`. Measured 2026-10-22 with an upper-bound probe (the last shadow ray of a path dropped, so the sample is splat and the next camera ray made in the same iteration; `LUXRAYS_KERNEL_LOAD_DIR`, PATHOCL 64 spp, two rounds): cornell 29.1 -> 29.0 ns/sample (-0.5%), classroom 64.4 -> 61.9 (-3.9%), kitchen 69.7 -> 67.1 (-3.6%). The real item keeps that shadow ray and splats its contribution from a pending record a separate film add, which has to agree with variance clamping, the adaptive half buffers, the AOV channels and Metropolis (whole-sample splats), so it would recover less than the bound: not worth it. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "B2".
- **B5. Triangle light sampling without repeats** `[DROPPED]`. Measured 2026-10-22 with an upper-bound probe: the light pick pdf of `DirectHitFiniteLight()` (the MIS weight of an emitter hit, which recomputes the learned slot probabilities and walks the tree) replaced by a constant. PATHOCL 64 spp, two rounds: cornell 29.1, classroom 64.5, manylights 7.6 ns/sample unchanged, kitchen 69.6 -> 67.0 (-3.7%). Recovering kitchen's share means storing the pick's slot probabilities (up to 16 floats) in the task state at every vertex of every learning scene, which the classroom would pay for nothing; removing the private `learned[]` array of `LightTree_SlotProbabilities()` changed nothing (the compiler had it). The solid-angle repeats are only in the opt-in `trianglelight.solidangle` mode (MC). *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "B5".
- **C. Occupancy re-assessment** (after A and B). *Re-run 2026-10-22 after B1 and A2 (PATHOCL 64 spp, two rounds): the kernel still sits at 256 VGPRs; `LUXRAYS_HIP_WAVES_PER_EU` 5 / 6 gives 192 / 160 VGPRs with more scratch and cornell 29.1 -> 34.4 / 31.7 ns/sample, classroom 64.5 -> 72.2 / 70-72, kitchen 69.7 -> 98.0 / 92.8: no free occupancy, what B1 removed was scratch, not live registers.* Re-run the `LUXRAYS_HIP_WAVES_PER_EU` sweep and the `-Rpass-analysis=kernel-resource-usage` dump (`hiprtc_remarks.py`). Multi-week; weigh it then, not now. *History:* PLAN G6, G7 ("rejected" paragraphs), G8 table.
- **G11b. Peak-depth bound of the material evaluation stack** `[PARTIAL]`. Done: the textures (peak depth of the op tree; the G0 Blender classroom 212 -> 21 floats, under the private 32). *The private variant for Mix scenes measured and dropped 2026-10-22:* kitchen (one Mix level, 86 floats by the sum) on private material stacks of 40 / 48 / 96 / 128 floats (`LUXRAYS_HIP_COMPILER_OPTIONS` defines, PATHOCL 64 spp, two rounds) 69.8 -> 80.0 / 85.3 / 90.4 / 90.3 ns/sample: a private stack that holds a Mix costs more scratch traffic than the global one saves (G11 measured +9.6% before G7 moved the BSDF and the temporaries to scratch). ### T3. Noise per sample: sampling and light transport

- **D1. Find the Cornell direct-light gap** (done 2026-10-23, measurement). Direct light only, the Blender Cornell box without its glass and metal, walls per-pixel var·spp from seed pairs: LuxCore 4.3x/9.6x/5.9x Cycles' at 64/256/1024 spp (270x270), flat from 64 to 256 spp (no stratification gain), whatever the strategy (tree, LOG_POWER, UNIFORM), on the CPU too, with blue noise off too. The cause is the area light exported as two triangle lights: the pick and the point use two Sobol dimensions that are a pair with t = 1-3, so each triangle's samples are poorly stratified (a toy: one triangle converges, the same light as two triangles does not). A kernel experiment sampling the quad as one spherical rectangle brings the walls to 1.1x/2.0x/1.13x of Cycles, unbiased; padded 2D dimensions as Cycles' close the 256 spp rest but lose at 64. Whole scene re-measured: Cycles 3.32x at equal error, noise per sample 0.98x (the 2.2x is gone), brightness 1.11/1.13/1.04 from the walls' Oren-Nayar and the light's temperature, which the addon did not export (fixed by F9; the light's Exposure is fixed). → D3. *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "D1"; PLAN G0 and light tree step 3.
- **D3. Quad lights sampled as one rectangle** (done 2026-10-23, both devices). Two triangles of a light mesh that make a planar parallelogram (every Blender area light, and the triangulated quads of user meshes) are one light for the direct light sampling of the path engines, found by the core (`lightstrategy.trianglelight.quad`, default on; not for BiDir): one pick, the importance of the quad (one leaf of the tree), the point from one 2D sample, by solid angle as a spherical rectangle (Ureña 2013) for a rectangle, by area otherwise; a BSDF hit on either triangle takes the quad's pdf. Means equal at depth 1, 3 and 9 against the quads off and BSDF-only renders on both devices (SDL box, Blender Cornell, classroom, kitchen, manylights with the DLS cache, the G0 classroom); the D1 probe's full-depth shift is not reproduced (its math rebuilt on HEAD is unbiased). Blender Cornell box: walls direct-only var·spp 0.097/0.085 -> **0.021/0.017** at 64/256 spp (Cycles 0.023/0.0083); the whole scene 1.105/1.147 -> 0.905/0.831, Cycles at equal error 3.3x -> **2.6x**; time per sample unchanged (kerneltiming: cornell, kitchen, classroom, manylights equal, G0 classroom +0.2%; VGPRs and scratch the same). Found on the way and fixed in the same work: the spherical rectangle's angles from atan2 (Cycles' acos loses 3e-4 rad near the plane), no upper solid-angle bound, a point within float precision of the quad's plane sampled by area (the classroom portal lit itself), the DLS cache's 2.5% floor on the second triangle (+17% on a box, +10% on manylights). BlendLuxCore: no change (the core pairs the triangles). *History:* SPEEDUP_CHANGES "D3".
- **D3b. Padded dimensions for the light point** `[MEASURE]`. The quad's two coordinates from Sobol dimensions 0 and 1 with their own index shuffle per vertex (Cycles' scheme; kernel experiment `lc-work/d3/kpad`): walls direct-only 0.0210/0.0169 -> 0.0217/0.0088 at 64/256 spp (Cycles 0.0233/0.0083), i.e. Cycles' figure at 256 spp but 3% worse at 64 (D1: 0.035 against 0.026 at 64). ### T6. Features and interop

- **F1. Ray-cone texture LOD, stochastic filtering, UDIM.** One cone width per path (Akenine-Möller et al. 2021) selects the mip level per lookup; the level is sampled stochastically with one texel fetch (Pharr et al. 2024); one float of path state, the same code on CPU and GPU; fixes the load-time single-level choice at `imagemap.cpp:918`. UDIM tokens in the loader on top. After A2. Measure equal-spp RMSE and samples/s on a scene with large `.tx` textures.
- **F2. Out-of-core texture streaming.** OIIO `TextureSystem`, CPU only (no open-source path tracer streams textures on the GPU). After F1.
- **F3. Light linking and shadow linking.** MoonRay's model: a *light set* (the lights that illuminate the object) and a *shadow set* (the lights whose shadow rays the object blocks) per geometry, both "all lights" by default. Properties `scene.objects.<name>.lightset` and `.shadowset`, lists of light names (an emissive mesh counts as one light), resolved to `lightSceneIndex` at compile time and stored as a bitmask on the object (`sceneobject.h:96-102`, `sceneobject_types.cl:26-35`, parsed at `parseobjects.cpp:181-185`, with the two object copy paths at `:264` and `:331`, compiled at `compilesceneobjects.cpp:34-65`); 64 linkable lights per scene is enough, more is an error at first. No per-object light mask exists today: `lightgroup` is the radiance-group index and `visibility` is the indirect-hit mask. Three places apply the light set: (1) the pick, `DirectLightSampling` at `pathtracer.cpp:171-186` and `DirectLight_Illuminate` at `pathoclbase_funcs.cl:305`: the light tree gets a per-node union mask (`LightTreeNode`, `lighttree_types.cl:37-54`) and a masked traversal in `LightTree_SampleLights` / `SampleLightPdf` (`lighttree_funcs.cl:425, 540`; the CPU tree calls the same `ocl::LightTree_*` functions, so one change covers both devices); the 1D distribution strategies reject unlinked picks and renormalize the pdf by the linked subset's weight sum, cached per distinct mask. (2) The emitter hit for MIS consistency, `DirectHitFiniteLight` / `DirectHitInfiniteLight` at `pathtracer.cpp:315-390` and `pathoclbase_funcs.cl:180, 227`: an emitter hit from a vertex on an unlinked object contributes nothing, so `EyePathInfo` (`pathinfo.h:93`, `pathinfo_types.cl:31`) needs the previous vertex's object. (3) The shadow set in the occlusion test next to the `cameraInvisible` skip, `scene.cpp:899` and `scene_funcs.cl:154`, with the shadow ray carrying its light index (the GPU already has it in `info`, `pathoclbase_funcs.cl:326-328`); `UpdateShadowBlockers()` (`scene.cpp:942-958`) must not count a shadow-linked object as a blocker, and the any-hit shadow fast path (`shadowRayAnyHit`, `pathoclbase.cpp:211-213`; the accelerators exit on the first hit, `bvh.cl:134`, `hiprtaccel.cl:100-116`) is off while any shadow set exists, which costs what a transparency scene costs today (V6's N-hit shadow rays restore it). BiDir, light tracing and PhotonGI apply both sets at the connection or the light-path hit (`Emit` callers: `pathtracer.cpp:1007`, `bidircputhread.cpp:633`, `tracephotonsthread.cpp:141`). Check: a scene with two lights and two objects each linked to one light: each object's pixels equal the single-light render within noise (seed pairs, both devices); a shadow set checked against the render without the blocker; `renderbench.py` for the any-hit loss. Addon: Blender's `Object.light_linking.receiver_collection` / `blocker_collection` (on the light) inverted into per-object sets at export (`export/light.py` and the object export); nothing exists in the addon today. 1-2 weeks.
- **F3b. Light filters.** MoonRay's light filters as a radiance multiplier on the non-intersectable lights: `scene.lights.<name>.filter.barndoor.{left,right,top,bottom,softness}` (edge angles in the light's local frame; spot, projection, point and sphere lights, the Z axis of `lightToWorld` as the direction), `.filter.cookie.{file,gain}` (an image map projected in the light's frame, through `imgMapCache` as the projection light at `parselights.cpp:390-396`, adding `IMAGEMAPS_EDIT` as at `:91-95`), `.filter.decay.{near.start,near.end,far.start,far.end}` (0 before the near start, 1 between, 0 after the far end, smoothstep ramps), `.filter.rod.{transformation,radius,edge,gain}` (a box region in which the light is scaled). Decay and rod depend on the receiving point and also apply to triangle and env lights later; barn door and cookie are direction-dependent. A `LightFilter` struct outside the param union in `NotIntersectableLightSource` (`light_types.cl:119-138`; CPU `light.h:155-192`), parsed with the common light properties at `parselights.cpp:464-476`, compiled with the common fields at `compilelights.cpp:359-366`. Applied in one place per device: the CPU `LightSource::Illuminate` becomes non-virtual and multiplies the per-type `IlluminateImpl` result, so the callers (`pathtracer.cpp:184`, `bidircputhread.cpp:476`) do not change; the GPU after the switch in `Light_Illuminate` (`light_funcs.cl:1051-1152`). On light paths (`Emit`: `pathtracer.cpp:1007`, `bidircputhread.cpp:633`, PhotonGI) the direction-dependent filters apply at `Emit`, the point-dependent ones at the first hit of the light ray. A filter only scales radiance, so the pick pdfs and MIS stay valid; the tree and power strategies see the unfiltered power, so picks inside a decayed region are wasted until the decay far range feeds the tree's bounds (`lighttree.cpp:541`), a later refinement. Check: a filter at identity (no cut, gain 1) bit-identical to the unfiltered render (the TILEPATHOCL list); barn doors against a spot plus four black quads (means equal); CPU/GPU parity on one scene per filter type; PATH against BiDir for the `Emit` side. Addon: a filter panel on the light (`properties/light.py`, `ui/light.py`, `export/light.py`); Blender's Eevee `use_custom_distance` / `cutoff_distance` maps to the decay far end, the rest is LuxCore-only UI. About a week plus the addon.

- **F7. Cryptomatte.** Film outputs `CRYPTOMATTE_OBJECT` and `CRYPTOMATTE_MATERIAL` in the standard layout (Psyop spec 1.2.0): per pixel N (hash, coverage) pairs sorted by coverage, N = 3 levels (6 floats, `film.outputs.<n>.levels`), the hash MurmurHash3_32 of the object or material *name*, and the EXR header attributes `cryptomatte/<id>/name`, `/hash = MurmurHash3_32`, `/conversion = uint32_to_float32`, `/manifest` (JSON name to hex hash). Today the ID channels are "last nearest wins" with no coverage (`filmaddsample.cpp:244-254`), the 24-bit IDs are packed into 8-bit RGB even for `.exr` (`filmoutput.cpp:565-599`), and the EXR writer takes one 3- or 4-channel `ImageBuf` with `chromaticities` as the only attribute (`:600-842, 846`): the output needs a named-multi-channel EXR writer (`CryptoObject00.R/G/B/A`, `CryptoObject01...`, plus the `CryptoObject.R/G/B` preview) and a manifest the film gets from the engine at init, since the film does not know the scene. Per-object and per-material name hashes compiled into the object (`sceneobject_types.cl:26-35`, `compilesceneobjects.cpp:34-65`) and the material, carried in the `SampleResult` at the first vertex like `objectID` (`pathtracer.cpp:600-601`, `pathoclbase_kernels_micro.cl:528-530`, `sampleresult.h:83-85`, `sampleresult_types.cl:39-41`). Accumulation in `AddSampleResultData` (`filmaddsample.cpp:222-267`, GPU `film_funcs.cl:280`): add the sample's filter weight to the slot with the same hash, else take a free slot, else replace the smallest if the new weight is larger; first version counts the first non-pass-through hit at full weight (transparent surfaces split their coverage later). The GPU slot update is a read-modify-write of a pixel several tasks can splat into: a per-pixel atomic CAS lock, cost measured with `renderbench.py`. New channel type in `film.h:143-192`, `filmchannels.cpp` (count, string), `film.cpp` `Resize` / `Clear` / `AddFilm` (a per-pixel slot merge, also what K5's resume needs), `CopyDynamicSettings`, the film serialization version, `filmparse.cpp:69` and `filmoutputs.cpp:60-370` (the `levels` property), `film_types.cl` flags and `FILM_PARAM` buffers, `compilefilm.cpp`, `ThreadFilm` (`pathoclbaseoclthreadfilm.cpp`), `luxcore.h` / `luxcoreimpl.cpp` / `pyluxcore.cpp` enums. Check: the per-pixel coverages sum to the alpha; the sum of one object's coverage over the image equals its `OBJECT_ID_MASK` count plus the edge pixels' shares; the EXR keys an object by name in Blender's compositor Cryptomatte node; CPU/GPU within noise. Addon: `view_layer.use_pass_cryptomatte_object/material` and `pass_cryptomatte_depth` in `export/aovs.py`, `engine.register_pass` for the `CryptoObject00..` passes and the manifest through `RenderResult.stamp_data_add_field` as Cycles does; nothing exists in the addon today. About a week plus 2-3 days of addon.
- **F8. Light path expression AOVs.** `film.outputs.<n>.type = LPE` with `.expression` in the OSL syntax (`C<RD>L`, `CS+DL`, `C<.D>.*L`; labels `L['name']` for a light and `O['name']` for an object), up to 8 expressions, each a float3 channel. The expression compiles to a DFA over the per-vertex alphabet (scatter D/G/S from the `BSDFEvent`, R/T, the object label) and the terminals L (light, with label), B (env light), O (emissive object); OSL's `lpexp` / `automata` files are BSD-3 and standalone (OSL is not in the deps, copy the two files), or a small own regex-to-DFA. Per-path state: one byte per expression in `EyePathInfo` (`pathinfo.h:93`, `pathinfo_types.cl:31`), advanced in `AddVertex` (`pathtracer.cpp:784`, `pathinfo_funcs.cl:76`, `pathoclbase_kernels_micro.cl:249`); at every contribution (`AddDirectLight` / `AddEmission`, `sampleresult.cpp:60-121`, `sampleresult_funcs.cl:89-176`, call sites `pathtracer.cpp:264, 357, 387`, `pathoclbase_funcs.cl:222, 278`, `pathoclbase_kernels_micro.cl:384-388`) the state is stepped by the terminal and the radiance added to the matching `SampleResult` entry. The GPU `SampleResult` sits in the task state, so the LPE entries exist only when LPE channels are on (the `SampleResult_Has*Channels` pattern, `sampleresult_funcs.cl:35-47`) and are capped at 4 there until measured; the DFA tables go in constant memory from `CompileFilm`. Film side as F7: the channel type, buffers, parse, output, HW buffers, enums. Second step: the fixed AOVs (`DIRECT_*`, `INDIRECT_*`, `EMISSION`, selected by `firstPathVertexEvent`) become built-in expressions, which removes that logic; light tracing and BiDir match the light subpath reversed at the connection. Check: on cornell and luxball-hdr with the deterministic TILEPATHOCL setup, `C<RD>L` plus `C<RG>L` plus `C<RS>L` plus their indirect forms equals the RGB channel to 1e-5 per pixel and each equals the matching fixed AOV; `L['g']` reproduces a radiance group; both devices. Addon: a list of named expressions on the view layer (`properties/view_layer.py`, `ui/view_layer_aovs.py`, `export/aovs.py`), each registered as a pass. 1-2 weeks.
- **F9. Blackbody temperature at luminance 1** (done 2026-10-23, both devices; decided by the user after D1). A third normalization of the blackbody `temperature` of the lights (`scene.lights.<n>.temperature.normalization`), of the emission (`scene.materials.<n>.emission.temperature.normalization`) and of the blackbody texture (`scene.textures.<n>.normalization`): `none`, `max` or `luminance`. When it is not set, the old bool `.normalize` picks `max` or `none`, so existing scenes render as before; `ToProperties()` writes `.normalization` only for `luminance`. `luminance` is Cycles' normalization (Blender 5.2's light Temperature and Blackbody node, read from `Light.temperature_color`): the Rec.709 colour of the blackbody spectrum, not desaturated into the gamut, scaled to luminance 1, converted to the working space, its negative components set to 0 (`WorkingColorSpace::FromTemperature()`, host code: one source for the CPU and the GPU). `max` divides by the largest RGB of any blackbody up to 13000 K: luminance 0.724 at 6500 K, 0.43 at 3000 K. Against Blender's colours (constant infinite light seen directly): within 0.1% per channel from 2000 to 12000 K, in Rec.709 and Rec.2020; at 1000 K red -0.4% and green +16% of 0.04 (the end of Cycles' fit); above 12000 K Cycles keeps the 12000 K colour, LuxCore follows the spectrum. Renders: the D1 Cornell box (Rec.2020, no glass or metal, Lambert walls), 270x270, direct light only, PATHOCL against Cycles on HIP, 256 spp x 2 seeds, LuxCore/Cycles of each wall's mean: a 6500 K light 1.000 in every channel (0.720-0.730 with `max`, which an older pyluxcore renders with the new addon; 0.97-1.01 with the temperature not exported, as before); a 3000 K light, a 3000 K Blackbody node in the light's nodes, and a mesh emitter with a 3000 K Blackbody node in its material (the blackbody texture): 0.999-1.001. At full depth (3000 K, depth 8) 0.985-0.997: the indirect light's own difference, not the temperature (a constant factor of the emission). Tests: 4 new in `testWorkingColorSpace.py` (luminance 1 and Blender's colours in each working space; `normalize` = `max` and the precedence of `.normalization`; the texture and the emission equal to the light; the export), all failing on the old build; the 13 tests of the file OK, HIP `--filter OCL` 169 OK, the CPU Light (70), Textures (5) and Material (115) tests OK. Parity against dba1bcd97 (TILEPATHOCL, every case and the G0 classroom): identical except the known luxball-hdr-vol pixel. Addon: the Cycles light Temperature and the Cycles Blackbody node (light nodes, materials) are exported with `luminance` and `.normalize = true`, so an older pyluxcore renders them with `max` instead of failing. Then (2026-10-24, the user's choice): the addon's own LuxCore Temperature (lights, world) and LuxCore Blackbody node have a Normalize option, Luminance or Max (Legacy); new ones and the Cycles-to-LuxCore node converter use Luminance, the lights, worlds and nodes of files from older addon versions get Max on load and append (BlendLuxCore `utils/temperature.py`; the exported scene of such a file is unchanged). D1 Cornell, 270x270, direct light, PATHOCL against Cycles HIP, 256 spp x 2 seeds: a converted 6500 K Blackbody emitter plane 0.999-1.000 per wall (3000 K the same), a LuxCore Temperature area light against a white one equal to Blender's light colour within 0.1% at 6500 K and 3000 K. Not changed: the white balance (luminance-normalized already) and the light-group temperature of the film. *History:* none (this entry).
