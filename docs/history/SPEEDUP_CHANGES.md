# Speeding PATHOCL up further: the levers left after G1-G11

> Superseded by [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) on 2026-10-21; kept for the done items and their measurements.

## Context

Cycles on the same RX 6950 XT is still ahead of PATHOCL. The last G0 re-measure (2026-10-17, light tree build, `MODERNIZATION_PLAN.md` Phase 2) puts Cycles at **4.5x** at equal error on the Blender Cornell box and **8.6x** on the Blender classroom. Splitting that into the two factors, from the G0 numbers and the samples/s history since:

| | Cornell box | classroom |
|---|---|---|
| time per sample, PATHOCL / Cycles | ~1.4x | ~3x |
| variance per sample, PATHOCL / Cycles | ~1.8x | ~0.85x |

So the two scenes have different problems. On a textured Blender scene the whole gap is **time per sample**, and it is the *shading* of a vertex: the current build does 203M rays/s on the 32-triangle Cornell box and 187M on the 247k-triangle classroom (`out/g9/bench-new-2.json`), so geometry and the HIPRT trace are not it. On a simple scene the gap is mostly **noise per sample** in the direct light, which the light tree did not close.

Three read-throughs of the current kernels (the fused `AdvancePaths_MK_RT`, the material/texture interpreter, the trace and host loop) found where a vertex spends its time today. The plan document's "fixed per-vertex overhead" diagnosis still holds, but the remaining overhead is now in identifiable places rather than in the state layout. The items below are ordered by expected gain per unit of work, with the two cheap measurements that decide between the first two tiers done first.

Key facts the plan relies on (all verified in the source at HEAD `5f57e1d8b`):

- **Every surface hit runs the material interpreter three times before shading** just to find the interior volume, the exterior volume and the pass-through transparency (`include/slg/bsdf/bsdf_funcs.cl:109-118`, `include/slg/scene/scene_funcs.cl:109`), each a `__noinline__` `Material_EvalOp` call with a second `__noinline__` per-material call and three switches, through the eval stack. For a transmitting material the interior walk repeats in `PathVolumeInfo_ContinueToTrace` and `PathVolumeInfo_Update`. The scene compiler already knows when a material's volumes are constant (`compilematerials.cpp:1099-1110` computes `shadowRaysNeedBSDF` from exactly these fields).
- **Textures are evaluated 2-3 times per vertex at the same hit point**: once in `Material_Evaluate` (direct light), once in `Material_Sample` (next ray), once in `Material_Albedo` when the AOV is on. Disney reads 11-14 textures per call, OpenPBR about 15 (`disney.cl:88-122`, `openpbr.cl:49-117`). Nothing is cached between the calls.
- **Image map lookups are software bilinear**: 4 taps, each a wrap switch with two integer modulos, a storage/channel switch and 3 scalar loads (`include/slg/imagemap/imagemap_funcs.cl:276-320, 401-450`); no mipmaps. There is no texture-object code in the HIP or CUDA devices (hipew has no `hipCreateTextureObject`). Cycles uses hardware texture objects on both.
- **`BSDF_Init` copies a 128-byte `Transform` into the hit point on every hit**, also for world-space meshes (`exttrianglemesh_funcs.cl:348-376`), always computes `dndu`/`dndv` (only generic procedural bump reads them), reads the vertex normals and UVs twice and the triangle indices 3-4 times. The private BSDF is ~352 B, the HitPoint ~300 B of it (`hitpoint_types.cl`, `bsdf_types.cl`).
- **The last vertex of every path costs an extra wavefront iteration**: with nothing to sample after it, the vertex falls back to the two-iteration flow (`pathoclbase_kernels_micro.cl:1029-1035`), the shadow ray is traced alone, and the splat and the next camera ray come one iteration later. A path of N vertices takes N+1 iterations; a path killed by Russian roulette goes through `MK_RT_DL_SPLAT` the same way.
- **The light-tree learning does 1 + slotCount contended float atomics per light pick plus one per visible light** (`lighttree_funcs.cl:626-674`), on by default; the plan recorded its cost at 8-10% of the GPU time.
- **Smaller state nits**: `task->seed` is read and written up to three times per iteration; `SampleResult_Init` writes all 8 radiance groups per path; the time and depth-of-field Sobol dimensions are drawn for every path even with a pinhole camera; in any-hit mode the shadow-ray pass-through dimension is still drawn and stored, and `ShadowRayHit` still multiplies the light radiance by white with a read-modify-write.
- **Direct light on a triangle light** recomputes the triangle's solid angle up to three times per sample (leaf importance, the `UseSolidAngle` test, the sample), re-reads the vertices from the mesh although the tree leaf stores them, runs a full `HitPoint_Init` plus a Transform copy on the light's temporary hit point, and the MIS pdf at the next vertex recomputes the slot probabilities the pick computed (`light_funcs.cl:484-601`, `lighttree_funcs.cl:540-617`).
- **Host side**: one `FinishQueue` per batch (fine), but every geometry or transform edit throws the whole `DataSet` away and rebuilds the HIPRT BVH at high quality on every device, plus an Embree BVH when native threads exist (`scenepreprocess.cpp:43-64`, `hiprtaccelhw.cpp:113-143`); all uploads are synchronous from pageable memory; `MergeThreadFilms` is single-threaded per `UpdateStats` poll; `opencl.native.threads.count` defaults to all hardware threads, which compete with the GPU feeder thread.
- Already as Cycles does it, no gain available: filter importance sampling of the pixel filter (one pixel per sample, `sampler_sobol_funcs.cl:239`), the Sobol-Burley per-dimension generation, one trace and one shading kernel per iteration, any-hit shadow rays.

## Step 0: two measurements that fix the order (half a day)

1. **Trace vs shading share on this machine.** No rocprof on Windows; add an opt-in `LUXRAYS_HIP_KERNEL_TIMING=1` that wraps each `hipModuleLaunchKernel` in `HIPDevice` with a pair of `hipEvent`s and accumulates time per kernel name, printed at thread end next to the existing transfer statistics. Run the six benchmark scenes. This replaces the Linux-only G8 profile and tells whether the trace is still 2-8% at 200M rays/s.
2. **How much of the classroom is textures.** Render the repo classroom and kitchen (and the G0 Blender classroom if its export is on this machine) with every image map texture replaced by its mean colour (a `--define`-style property or a small Python scene rewrite), same spp, and compare the time per sample. The difference bounds the gain of A1 + A2 together; if it is under 15%, A3/A4 (the interpreter) come before A2.

## Tier A: shading cost on textured scenes (the classroom's 3x)

**A1. Evaluate each texture once per vertex.** A per-vertex texture value cache: a small private array (8-16 entries of texture index + float3, scratch on HIP) owned by the kernel, cleared at each new vertex, passed to `Texture_GetFloatValue` / `Texture_GetSpectrumValue` and consulted on the IMAGEMAP fast path and the slow path before evaluating; the light's `GetEmittedRadiance` and the next vertex use their own hit point so they bypass it. First step: image map textures only (the expensive ones). Second step, if the first pays: a material "parameter record" filled once per hit, shared by Evaluate and Sample (Cycles' closure model; also the CPU item C6). Bit-identical. Files: `include/slg/textures/texture_funcs.cl`, `texture_imagemap_funcs.cl`, `pathoclbase_kernels_micro.cl` (the cache lives beside the private BSDF), the `TEXTURES_PARAM` macros. Expected: on a matte image-map scene 2 of 3 lookups go away.

**A2. Hardware texture sampling on HIP and CUDA.** Upload image maps as texture objects (`hipCreateTextureObject` / `cuTexObjectCreate`, linear filtering, the wrap mode of the map, normalized coordinates, RGB byte/half maps padded to RGBA), keep the 64-bit handles in a per-page handle buffer referenced from the `ImageMap` descriptor, and make `ImageMap_GetSpectrum` / `GetFloat` / `GetDuv` a `tex2D` on CUDA/HIP with the software path kept for OpenCL and the CPU. New hipew/cuew entries. The unit-test images will differ at the last bits (hardware 8-bit filter weights), so the comparison tolerance applies. Expected: a bilinear RGB lookup goes from ~12 scalar loads plus 8 integer modulos and two switches to one instruction; it is also the prerequisite of the ray-cone mipmap item. Memory +33% for RGB byte maps. Files: `src/luxrays/devices/hipdevice.cpp`, `cudadevice.cpp`, `deps/hipew`, `deps/cuew`, `src/slg/engines/pathoclbase/compileimagemaps.cpp`, `include/slg/imagemap/imagemap_funcs.cl`, `imagemap_types.cl`.

**A3. Skip the three interpreter walks per hit when the material's answer is constant.** In `compilematerials.cpp`, per material: `constInteriorVolume` / `constExteriorVolume` (the material is not Mix, glossycoating or two-sided with differing children, nor pass-through dependent) and `hasPassThroughTransparency` (the per-material version of the `shadowRaysNeedBSDF` test). `BSDF_Init` and `Scene_Intersect` read the index or the flag directly and only fall into `Material_GetInteriorVolume` / `GetExteriorVolume` / `GetPassThroughTransparency` for the materials that need the walk; same for the two re-walks in `pathvolumeinfo_funcs.cl:143,190`. Bit-identical, one day, every scene. Also `Frame_ToLocal(fixedDir)` computed once per vertex instead of three times (`bsdf_funcs.cl:279-348`).

**A4. A no-stack fast path for one-op materials.** Every leaf material (matte, Disney, OpenPBR, glass, ...) compiles to exactly one op per call, but still pays the push/pop through the eval stack (global memory on any scene with a Mix) and two `__noinline__` calls with three switches. In `Material_Evaluate` / `Sample` / `Albedo`: when `evalOpLength == 1`, call the material's evaluate function directly with register arguments. Mix and the other multi-op materials keep the interpreter. Bit-identical. Measure on cornell (all matte) and on the Blender classroom.

**A5. Common subexpression sharing in the texture op compiler.** `compiletextures.cpp:333+` flattens the texture graph depth-first with no sharing, so a texture referenced twice (Blender graphs do this constantly: one image feeding colour, roughness and bump through math nodes) is evaluated twice; the Blender classroom compiles to 7164 texture ops. Memoize per texture index inside one evaluation: a slot area at the bottom of the texture eval stack, written by the first evaluation and read by a new `COPY_SLOT` op. Reduces both the op count and the stack size, so more scenes fit the G11 private stacks. Only after A1 shows what the texture share is.

## Tier B: fixed per-vertex overhead on every scene

**B1. HitPoint diet.** Store a mesh index and read the `Transform` on demand only for instanced and motion meshes (world-space meshes get the identity path, no copy); compute `dndu`/`dndv` lazily inside the generic bump path; read the triangle indices, normals and UVs once in `HitPoint_Init` and pass them to `GetDifferentials`; the triangle light's temporary hit point gets a cut-down init (it only needs `geometryN`, `intoObject` and the UV of the emission texture). Target: the private BSDF from ~352 B to ~200 B, less scratch traffic and fewer live values. Bit-identical. Files: `hitpoint_types.cl`, `hitpoint_funcs.cl`, `exttrianglemesh_funcs.cl`, `bsdf_funcs.cl`, `light_funcs.cl`.

**B2. No extra iteration at the end of a path.** Cycles' shadow path: at the last vertex, sample the next *camera* ray in the same kernel run and trace it together with the last shadow ray, keeping the finished sample's pending direct-light contribution in a small record (pixel index, radiance per light group, the few AOV members with a channel) that the shadow-ray result completes and splats in the next iteration, while the new path already shades its first vertex. Same for the Russian-roulette end (`MK_RT_DL_SPLAT`). Saves one of N+1 iterations per path; the contribution order per sample changes, so the check is statistical (seed-pair variance and means), not bit-identity. The drain at the halt (`DrainPaths`) must wait for the pending records. Files: `pathoclbase_kernels_micro.cl` (states and the end-of-path block at 1092-1228), `pathoclbase_datatypes.cl`, `pathoclbaseoclthreadinit.cpp` (the record buffer).

**B3. Cheaper light-tree learning.** Three options, measured in that order, all unbiased since the learning only steers the pick probabilities: stop accumulating in a cell once its weight has saturated (`N/(N+64)` capped at 0.8 is reached at 256 samples: a `done` flag per cell skips the atomics); learn from one sample in four (a hash of the task and pass); wave-level aggregation before the atomic (same cell and slot across lanes is the common case on a wall). Expected: most of the 8-10%. Files: `lighttree_funcs.cl:626-674`, `pathoclbaseoclthread.cpp:412-437`.

**B4. State traffic nits** (one commit, bit-identical): read `task->seed` once per kernel run and write it once; `SampleResult_Init` clears only the radiance groups the film has; skip the time and lens dimensions for a pinhole static camera; in any-hit mode skip the shadow-ray pass-through dimension and the white multiply in `ShadowRayHit`; in `SampleResult_Init` and `GenerateEyePath` the double albedo clear and the double `PathVolumeInfo_Init`. 1-3%.

**B5. Triangle light sampling without repeats**: take the vertices from the tree leaf, compute the solid angle once per sample and pass it on, and let the pick return the slot probabilities for the MIS pdf of the next vertex instead of recomputing them (`DirectHitFiniteLight`). Bit-identical up to float order. Matters on the Cornell-like scenes where direct light is most of the vertex.

## Tier C: occupancy (structural, after A and B)

The fused kernel sits at 256 VGPRs and 2496 B of scratch, 4 waves per SIMD; Cycles' HIP kernels are capped at 64 registers with a small SoA state and five small kernels per bounce. The G7 experiments showed that copying the state to private memory spills and that a register cap without removing live state loses 5-23%. A1-A4 and B1 remove code and live values from the kernel, which is the only thing that lowers the natural register count; after them, re-run the `LUXRAYS_HIP_WAVES_PER_EU` sweep (the hook exists) and the `-Rpass-analysis=kernel-resource-usage` dump. If the kernel then fits 5-6 waves without spilling, the gain comes for free; if not, the remaining route is the per-member SoA accessor refactor plus per-state kernels with queues (G6), a multi-week item to weigh then, not now.

## Tier D: noise per sample on simple scenes (the Cornell 1.8x)

**D1. Find the direct-light noise gap.** The G0 direct-only measurement put the walls at 8-14x Cycles' variance per sample even with the tree and solid-angle sampling, and 77% of it was the light seen through the glass sphere, i.e. a path-depth semantics difference in the experiment, not a sampling one. Redo the experiment cleanly: the Blender Cornell box without the glass and metal spheres, direct light only in both engines, seed pairs at 64 spp, per-pixel var·spp on the walls. If a gap remains, bisect it with: the point on the light drawn from two Sobol dimensions instead of one dimension split by its even and odd bits (`sphericaltriangle_funcs.cl`, the layout has the dimensions); the light as one quad versus two triangles (the tree's 50/50 cut); the MIS weights (BSDF-only and light-only renders). One day of measurement; it decides whether a sampler item follows.

**D2. Time to a clean image.** GPU OIDN and the Cycles-style adaptive halt as the default are already in the plan (blocked on the OIDN device modules); nothing new here.

## Tier E: time to first pixel and the viewport (Blender)

The G0 fixed cost per render was 0.85 s (Cornell) to 4.3 s (classroom) against 0-0.3 s for Cycles; a Blender user iterating on a scene feels this more than 10% of samples/s.

- **E1. Edits without a full rebuild.** A transform edit today rebuilds the whole `DataSet`, the HIPRT scene at high quality on every device, and the Embree BVH for the native threads. Keep the HIPRT geometries across transform edits and rebuild only the instance scene (`hiprtaccelhw.cpp`), use the `Balanced` build for RTPATHOCL, and skip the Embree build when `opencl.native.threads.count = 0`. Then break down the remaining startup (scene compile, synchronous pageable uploads, light tree build, image map upload) with timers and make the uploads asynchronous from pinned memory.
- **E2. Native CPU threads.** The default of all hardware threads next to the GPU competes with the feeder thread and, per the open issues, is where the intermittent crashes are. Check what BlendLuxCore exports; default to 0 for PATHOCL unless hybrid is asked for, or reserve one core per GPU (Phase 4 item 2).
- **E3. Parallel film merge.** `AddFilmImpl` over TBB, so a 4K `UpdateStats` poll does not take a single-threaded 100+ ms under the film mutex.

## Execution order and what each commit is checked against

1. Step 0 (the two measurements) and B3 option (a) (a day): cheap and they fix the order below.
2. A3, B4, B1: bit-identical items, each a commit, checked with TILEPATHOCL at a fixed 64 spp on the ten parity scenes against the previous build and the HIP OCL suite; speed with `renderbench.py` alternating runs (rays/s on Windows, ±1%).
3. A1 (image maps first), then A2 if Step 0.2 says the textures are a large share; A4 and A5 after, measured on cornell and the classroom.
4. B2 and B5: statistically identical items, checked with seed pairs (var·spp at 64 and 256 spp) and equal means on cornell, kitchen, luxball-hdr-vol and the shadow-catcher scene; the halt drain checked on the subsurface scene.
5. D1 in parallel with any of the above (measurement only).
6. Tier C re-assessment with the waves sweep; Tier E as a separate Blender-facing track.

After each commit: the addon check of `CLAUDE.md` (none of these items adds a scene property, except a possible `pathocl.lighttree.learning.*` tweak and the texture-object toggle, which default on), and the addon rebuild/install with a fresh wheel.
